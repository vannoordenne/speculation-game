Digital Puppeteer

Emotion Recognition Children Subscription Learning Challenges Educate

An educational app watches children’s faces and voices during lessons, adapts the content in real time, and sells parents detailed mood reports as a monthly subscription.

Why it’s dark tech

Care for learning becomes continuous emotional surveillance. Children can’t meaningfully consent, parents pay to keep watching, and “better education” legitimises data extraction from a vulnerable group.

Silent Streets

Ambient Surveillance Citizens Government Funding Safety Risks Monitor

A city-wide sensor network builds behaviour profiles of every citizen and sends predictive alerts to authorities whenever someone looks “suspicious” according to the model.

Why it’s dark tech

Public safety funds a system that treats everyone as a risk score. Bias in the model becomes police action, opting out is impossible, and civic space turns into a soft form of social control.

MemoryMarket

Brain-Computer Interfaces Older Adults Pay-Per-Use Health Challenges Support

A service extracts and stores older adults’ memories “for care,” then sells family members pay-per-use access, including AI recreations of relatives after they die.

Why it’s dark tech

Support for cognitive decline becomes a market in grief and identity. Consent is blurry when people are vulnerable, ownership of a life’s memories sits with a platform, and mourning is productised.
